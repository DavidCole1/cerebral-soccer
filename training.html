On the pitch

Training that respects the game

I build each session around what the game actually asks: clean technique, awareness before the ball arrives, and the confidence to make decisions under pressure.

1-on-1

Private training

Private training gives us the time to slow the game down, find the details that matter, and build a plan around one player.

The first session is an evaluation. I watch how you receive, strike, move, and make decisions, then identify the two or three changes that can make the biggest difference. Every session after that has a clear goal and finishes with work you can continue on your own.

  • Individual development plan after the first session
  • Position-specific technical & tactical work
  • Progress check-ins parents actually understand
  • Training homework between sessions

2 to 4 players

Small group training

Some things need an opponent. Small group sessions add pressure, competition, and the quick choices that decide real games while keeping the group small enough for individual coaching.

I can place your player with others at a similar age and level, or you can bring a teammate, sibling, or a few friends from your club.

  • Groups matched by age and level
  • 1v1, 2v2 and small-sided decision games
  • Constant individual correction within the group
  • Bring-your-own-group option

How I train

Passing, receiving, and dribbling sit at the center of the work because every player relies on them, no matter the position.

  1. Stations Three stations, one plan

    Three focused stations give us enough repetition to improve without letting the session become predictable.

  2. Tactics The bigger picture in every drill

    Every drill includes a game picture, so players understand when and why to use the technique.

  3. Scanning Decision-making under pressure

    Players learn to scan before the ball arrives, recognize their options, and make the next decision sooner.

Questions

Training FAQ

Where do sessions take place?

Sessions take place at fields in and around Alexandria and Northern Virginia. Once we set a schedule, I'll confirm the exact location and keep the travel reasonable for your family.

How often should my player train?

Most players do well with one session a week alongside their club schedule. Two sessions can make sense before tryouts or a move to a new level. After the evaluation, I'll tell you what I honestly think will help.

What does it cost?

Rates depend on whether the training is private or small group and how often we meet. Send an inquiry and I'll include the current rate sheet so you can decide whether it fits.

Do you work with goalkeepers?

My specialty is field-player development. Goalkeepers are welcome to work on distribution, footwork, and playing out of the back. For dedicated shot-stopping work, I'll point you to a goalkeeper specialist.

My player already trains with their club. Why add this?

Club training has to serve the whole team. Individual work gives your player more purposeful touches, direct feedback, and time to focus on the details their position demands. I can also align the work with what their club coach is asking from them.
